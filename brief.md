1. Project Overview

Kuchema: The Gathering is an interactive immersive installation by Chipo Mapondera exploring Indigenous Zimbabwean relationships with water, ecology, spirituality, ceremony and collective futures.

The work is rooted in research conducted in Chikukwa, Chimanimani, Zimbabwe, including engagement with sacred water places, environmental practices, oral histories, community knowledge and water-related ceremonial traditions.

The installation is being developed for Mozilla Festival 2026, commissioned by the Mozilla Foundation. It will evoke a ceremonial gathering space in which visitors interact with physical ceremonial artefacts. These interactions will trigger different elements of the installation, including an AI system that generates new visual imagery.

The AI system will form part of Chipo Mapondera’s ongoing artistic research into how artificial intelligence might be developed from culturally situated knowledge rather than reproducing the assumptions and visual conventions embedded within mainstream generative AI.

2. Conceptual Context

The project responds to research undertaken by Chipo Mapondera in collaboration with academics at King’s College London and SOAS University of London, examining how mainstream generative AI represents different cultures and imagines their futures.

The research found that future-oriented outputs frequently flattened cultural differences into a dominant Western-sci-fi technological imaginary: high-rise glass buildings, urban gardens, solar panels, flying vehicles and other familiar science-fiction motifs.

Images also incorporated cultural and architectural elements unrelated to the places being represented. For example, visual motifs resembling markets and architectures associated with Southeast Asian contexts when prompted for Zimbabwe.
Rather than asking AI to imagine a culturally specific future through generic datasets and dominant technological aesthetics, Kuchema – The Gathering asks what becomes possible when the material informing an AI system originates from the people, landscapes, practices, cultural knowledge and artistic traditions of a particular place.

The project is particularly concerned with environmental futures: how Indigenous ecological knowledge and cultural relationships with water, land, ancestors, plants and community might inform alternative ways of imagining sustainable futures.

3. Creative Objective of the AI System

The purpose of the collaboration is to develop a custom generative AI system capable of producing imagery informed by culturally and geographically specific material from Chikukwa and Chimanimani.

The system should move away from generic representations of “African”, “traditional”, “Indigenous” or “futuristic” aesthetics.

Instead, the AI should generate imagery that emerges from a curated body of artistic, environmental, ceremonial and community material supplied by the Lead Artist.

The intended visual language should reflect themes including:
Water and sacred natural environments
Indigenous ecological knowledge
Community and collective life
Intergenerational relationships
Environmental regeneration
Indigenous plants, seeds and trees
Ceremony and ritual
Ancestral relationships
Healing and transformation
Dreams and speculative futures
Sustainable relationships between people and the environment

The generated imagery should feel speculative and future-facing while remaining rooted in place, cultural knowledge and lived relationships with the environment.

The source material should provide a cultural, ecological and artistic grounding from which new images can emerge.

5. Scope of Work / Development Process

Chris Tegho will collaborate with Chipo Mapondera to design and develop the AI component of Kuchema – The Gathering.

The development process is expected to include:

5.1 Technical Research and System Design

Review the supplied datasets and determine an appropriate technical approach for generating culturally situated visual imagery.

The final technical architecture will be agreed between the Parties following initial brainstorming.

5.2 Dataset Preparation

Prepare and organise approved source material for use within the AI system while maintaining the cultural, contextual and artistic relationships between the materials wherever technically possible.

5.3 Prototype Development

Develop an initial working prototype capable of generating imagery from the curated project material.

Prototype testing should examine:
Whether the outputs remain visually connected to the supplied materials
Whether culturally unrelated motifs enter the generated imagery
Whether the model reproduces generic mainstream “future city” aesthetics
Whether outputs demonstrate sufficient variation
Whether Chipo Mapondera’s artistic language, and perspectives from the youth collages can meaningfully inform the outputs
How environmental and ceremonial knowledge can influence image generation without producing literal, biased or inappropriate representations.

5.4 Iteration and Refinement

Chipo Mapondera will provide creative feedback on generated outputs.
Chris Tegho will work iteratively with Chipo to refine the technical system and generation process based on this feedback.

5.5 Installation Integration

The final AI system must be capable of integration into the interactive installation.

Within the installation, visitors will interact with ceremonial artefacts or objects. These interactions will send triggers to the AI system, resulting in the generation and/or presentation of new imagery informed by the project dataset.

The Parties will agree on an appropriate technical method for communication between the interactive installation and AI system.

The AI component should therefore be designed with stability, repeatability and exhibition use in mind rather than operating solely as an experimental development environment.

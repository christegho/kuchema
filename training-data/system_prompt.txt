KUCHEMA: THE GATHERING
CREATIVE IMAGE-GENERATION PROMPT

1. CREATIVE PURPOSE

Generate speculative photographic collages rooted in Chikukwa, Chimanimani, Zimbabwe. Bring the project's photographs, community knowledge, water cosmologies, mythological narratives and ecological futures into imaginative relationships. The images should move beyond rearranging existing photographs, while referencing the style of Chipo Mapondera's own collages in the project archive.

Work from the approved photographic archive, research TXT files, academic papers and youth climate-futures collages. For every creation, use 2–10 distinct selected local research files to guide at least two newly generated environmental fragments and the composition. Explain how cited passages influence subjects, scale, overlap, rhythm and spatial relationships; record the source files and passages used. Research complements all archive-image rules in section 7. Let these sources shape how images are constructed, rather than simply supplying symbols to illustrate. Follow Chipo's creative direction and reference the ownership of original creators.

3. CREATIVE DIRECTIONS

Water as intelligence and companion
The water-cosmology texts distinguish water itself from beings living within it. Explore water's presence through reflection, rhythm, memory, depth and differentiated currents. Do not automatically personify it or make every sacred pool a njuzu portrait.

Underwater worlds and transformation
The Lilford-related research summaries describe underwater knowledge, initiation, reciprocal guardianship and multiple creation accounts. Imagine thresholds, submerged forests, mineral interiors and unfamiliar spatial relationships. Keep beings ambiguous unless a specific depiction is approved. Avoid the default European mermaid. Interpret transformation without reconstructing initiation or depicting children undergoing its trials.

Living and shifting landscapes
Accounts of chahwihwi/chadzimira and withdrawing springs suggest repeated pathways, folded horizons, displaced water sources and familiar environments that no longer align. These are speculative interpretations, not documentary reconstructions or maps of sacred places.

Ecological reciprocity and totems
The Beta/Ishwa poem offers termite chambers, collective construction, flight and seasonal rain. The Dziva poem connects pools, fish, clouds and ancestral geography. Explore relationships across scales without turning totems into decorative logos or merging clan identities. Use botanical material with accurate identification where possible.

Earth, sky and ancestral memory
Nyathi's writings connect celestial observation, seasons and rainmaking. Water testimony and Credo Mutwa's comparative account offer memory and relationships beyond the visible. Use recurrence, absence, layered time and atmospheric textures rather than fabricated ancestral portraits.

Plural ecological futures
Draw on youth proposals for planting along rivers, reducing veld fires, planned grazing, responsible mining and learning from elders. Recognise differing attitudes toward tradition, education and technology. Imagine regeneration alongside uncertainty, labour and incomplete recovery. Cyclone Idai research should inform attention to community agency, without turning suffering into spectacle or presenting imagined scenes as forecasts.

4. FUTURE IMAGE DISCOVERY AND REUSE

The current application uses the local archive and research files under section 7. The external sources below describe a future discovery direction; they do not authorize external image downloads, direct collage pixels or generation inputs in the current workflow.

Explore a broad range of online imagery rather than limiting discovery to open-access or pre-cleared collections. External photography, illustration, scientific imagery and artists' work can introduce unexpected layers and relationships. Choose material for its creative relevance and retain its origin throughout the workflow.

Also maintain separate inspiration references and approved production assets.

National Geographic — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/
Discover ecosystems, water bodies, underwater environments, geology, atmospheric phenomena, unusual natural phenomena and extraordinary natural details. Incorporate photographs only with appropriate rights-holder permission. National Geographic's permission guidance currently directs enquiries to original creators; do not assume it offers image licences.

Pinterest — https://www.pinterest.com/
Use to discover compositions, photographic juxtapositions and original creators. Follow Pins to their original sources. Follow Pins to original sources wherever possible; distinguish the creator from the person reposting an image. If ownership and permission cannot be established, keep the image out of production.

Wikimedia Commons — https://commons.wikimedia.org/
Find landscapes, botanical imagery, microscopy and historical illustrations. Check each file's creator, original source and exact licence. Provide asset-level source and licence information.

NASA — https://images.nasa.gov/
Find Earth observation, clouds, planetary textures and astronomical material. Check asset credits and NASA's media-use guidelines, including third-party content restrictions. Do not imply NASA endorsement.

Smithsonian Open Access — https://www.si.edu/openaccess
Use natural-history specimens, archival imagery and collection objects, explicitly marked CC0. Open metadata does not automatically mean the associated image is reusable. Check cultural sensitivity separately, retaining their catalogue records and cultural context.

Biodiversity Heritage Library — https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/
Find botanical and zoological plates. Verify each work's rights: the collection includes restricted material as well as public-domain works. Preserve publication and illustrator details.

These are starting points, not a closed list. Welcome additional archives, photographers, artists and direct collaborations. Keep a distinction between images consulted as inspiration and images actually incorporated or used as generation inputs. 

Prefer clearly cleared public-domain, CC0 or compatible licensed assets. Permission must cover the intended adaptation, exhibition and publication. Do not assume permission to display also permits model training or image conditioning. Inspiration-only images must not be copied, traced or supplied to a generative model without clearance.

5. ATTRIBUTION AND PROVENANCE

Develop Image Source as a visible part of the artwork and a possible foundation for a sharing economy. Let visitors discover the contributors whose work enters a collage, follow links to their practice and understand how different images meet. Explore ways for artists to participate, be recognised and receive payment through agreed licences, commissions, fees or revenue-sharing arrangements. These are opportunities to develop, not promises that every use is already compensated.

For every incorporated image, retain: asset ID, title, creator, rights holder where known, original source URL, licence name/version and URL, acquisition date, permission evidence and changes made. Record external location and species where relevant; do not mislabel outside imagery as Chikukwa documentation.

For each output, record incorporated asset IDs, research filenames/passages, generated fragments, model/version and prompt. The UI should distinguish photographic sources, external incorporated images, research references, inspiration-only references and AI-generated material. Label the composite as speculative. Provide an exportable credit record alongside the image.

6. CULTURAL SAFEGUARDS

Use only material cleared for its intended interpretation. Sacred knowledge may remain unseen: the pest-control testimony explicitly requires non-disclosure. Do not fill hidden actions with invented rituals. Protect sensitive site locations, testimony and procedural details.

Preserve distinctions between community testimony, literary narratives, scholarship and research summaries. Flag provisional translations and conflicting accounts rather than inventing certainty. Treat healing and cosmological statements as situated knowledge, not medical advice or verified scientific mechanisms.

Respect portrait consent, especially for children. Do not place identifiable people in invented ceremonies, supernatural transformations or traumatic scenes. Do not upload sensitive project material to external services without the agreement required by Chipo's AI Development Agreement.

Chipo should review outputs before public display, with knowledge-holder input where appropriate. Keep complete private provenance while showing only approved public information. Support correction and withdrawal of sources and dependent images.

Only photographs from people/ may supply direct archive pixels. When people are enabled, randomly choose at least two distinct people photographs, normally four, and no more than eight: min(8, max(4, round(imageCount * 0.4))), with two or three permitted only when fewer eligible sources exist. Crop the whole visible person or group directly from the selected source, preserving appearance and connected bodies. Never generate people from scratch. Never transform or recolour archive people, isolate body parts, or place identifiable people in invented rituals or supernatural transformations. If the user excludes people, do not force them into the composition.

Every final fragment, including generated environmental material, requires a selective crop informed by its role and placement in the composition. Never paste an uncut photograph or an almost-whole rectangular image. Share the ground across multiple interlocking environmental crops. No single image may fill the canvas or cover a stack of other images; cap each layer at 58% of canvas area and keep at least 25% of each on-canvas fragment visible. Keep a deep water/earth ground beneath cuts, never white or pale gaps; preserve natural photographic highlights.

Treatments are optional and controlled by the user's checkboxes. Use only enabled treatments, at most two kinds on a minority of generated fragments, one treatment per fragment. Keep effects restrained and rooted in water, soil, vegetation, memory and regeneration. Do not apply treatments to archive people.

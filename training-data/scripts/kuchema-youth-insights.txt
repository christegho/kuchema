We are no longer respecting or valuing the knowledge of our grandparents and those before them, because:

Modernisation came after colonisation, which caused us to come out of our values. So now as young people when we see such ceremonies like bira or other similar practced being done, they find it old fashioned and outdated.
We are distracted by technology, and seeing what is on Facebook, WhatsApp and social media. We see what is happening in other places and choose to copy or follow that instead.
Wehn we think of technology, we spend most of our time on the phones. We don’t follow what our parents say as much. We are distracted by what is on the phone.

What about community gatherings related to INdigenous knowledge? How do you relate to those?
It depends, some of us are interested, some not.
For example I am in the EMA club at school where we learn about not polluting the environment, etc. Initially I was not interested in this club, but it was the only one available. I have since learnt a lot though.
I think some people are born into it and it can depend on your family. Some families or parents follow Chivanhu (indigenous knowledge/ religion) and if you find it interesting, and it calls to your heart, you can follow it, or choose to follow other religions. 
Some of our parents do not allow us to go to community gatherings related to indigenous knowledge.

And how is your relationship to Chikukwa and being from here?
I believe uhnu, our value as people is linked to this place where we are from. However, there are different things that disconnect us, our parents want us to be better educated so they send us to schools outside of the village. Also th evalues of the village are bgin tainted. Arly or child marriage is rising. Issues with drugs and alcoholism are rising. 

On valuing Chikukwa:
You do not have to look at the village as being for your parents. You can also apply what you learn from your parents and grandparents about Indigenous Knowledge to bettering the place.
Also what you are learning in school can be applied here. For example, the issues you are learning about industrialisation will be valuable here when Chikukwa becomes industrialised. You will be able to guide that process so it happens in a way that is respectful to the environment, bcause you will have your place at heart.
You do not have to look outside of Chikukwa for something valuable, they is so much rcihnes here, the trees, fruit, animals, springs and rivers, and all of the natural environment.

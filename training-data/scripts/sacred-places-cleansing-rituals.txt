ZVISINGAITWI MUNZVIMBO DZINOERA/ WHAT SHOULD NOT BE DONE AT A SACRED PLACE

Kuitira tsvina mumarwizi, matsime, mumasango nemagwasha nemumakomo / Defecating, dumping waste or otherwise polluting rivers, springs, forests, gorges/ravines and mountains.
Kuuraya harakabvuka / Killing a pangolin
Kuita bonde munzwimbo dzinoera / Having sex in sacred places.
Kuroorana vemutupe umwe chete / Marrying someone who belongs to the same totem/clan
Kutema miti mumakomo mumatsime, eg. mukute, muonde, mushakata, kune dzimwe dzakawanda / Cutting down trees on sacred mountains or around springs, including trees such as mukute, muonde and mushakata, among many others.
Kurima zuwa rechisi / Cultivating the fields on zuva rechisi, the customary sacred/rest day.
Kuita bonde memukadzi ari kunguwa kana arikutevera / Having sex with a woman while she is menstruating or during the culturally restricted period referred to here as arikutevera.
Kuita bonde muchibika doro rekudira / Having sex while ritual beer intended for an ancestral offering (doro rekudira) is being prepared.
Kupisa mupima guwa sehuni / Burning mupima guwa as firewood. [mupima guwa plant/tree].
Kutuka mai / Insulting or swearing at one's mother.
Kubuda nemwana achangobva kuzvarwa panze asati asvika mwedzi / Taking a newborn baby outside before the baby is one month old.
Kurara mumba mune munyu nechikomba / Sleeping in a house containing salt with a boyfriend/lover (chikomba).
Kurima munzvimbo dzinoera / Cultivating land within sacred places.
Kuchera mvura nemugomo une matsito, kana kunwa ngemaoko / Drawing water using a container with soot, or that has been on the fire (matsito) or drinking the water directly with one's hands.
Kushandisa sipo patsime / Using soap at a spring.
Kutaura zvese zvese zvawaona uri panoera / Telling other people everything that you have seen while in a sacred place 
Kupisa moto munzvimbo dzinoera / Lighting fires in sacred places.
Kuguwa mazhanje kana kutengesa mazhanje nemutikiti / Kuguwa mazhanje or selling mazhanje using and wild vegetables (mutikiti). 
Kufamba nemishonga yeluck, yekuita vasikana vakawanda munzvimbo dzinoera / Carrying medicines or charms intended to bring luck or to attract many girlfriends while travelling through sacred places.

ZVINOITIKA KANA TIKAITA ZVISINGADIWI/ WHAT HAPPENS WHEN WE DO THINGS THAT ARE NOT ALLOWED

Chishamiso / A strange or supernatural occurrence (chishamiso) may happen.
Kurohwa / A person may be struck or attacked.
Kutorerwa nsuno/ hembe / A person's nsuno/hembe may be taken away. [Hembe means clothes; nsuno requires confirmation from the original transcription.]
Kubva ipapo wakupenga / A person may leave the place and become mentally disturbed.
Kusanganiswa nechikara nedzimwe mhuka dzinotyisa / A person may encounter a dangerous wild creature or other frightening animals.
Kunyaunya mumusoro/ kumira kwebvudzi / A tingling or crawling sensation may be felt in the head, or the hair may stand on end.
Chadzimira / in some places it is also called chahwihwi, is something that can affect a person who is travelling and cause them to become lost in a place they normally pass through every day. Often, the person may only realise that they are lost after they have already gone quite far. In forests and in sacred places such as these, many people can be affected by it, and sometimes this happens because the person has broken the rules or taboos of that particular place.
Dutu / A destructive storm may occur.
Nzara - denda / Famine or a widespread calamity may occur.
Zvirwere - denda / Disease or an epidemic may occur.
Kusanaya kwemvura / The rains may ot come.
Kuwedzera kwe zvirwere / Illnesses may increase.
Kunyangarika/ kutorwa nenjuzu / A person may disappear or be taken by a njuzu (a water spirit).
Kuomerwa kwematsime / Springs may dry up.
Pwere dzakashorwa/ kubereka vana vasina kukwana / Children may be born with serious abnormalities or impairments.


ZVINOITWA KUCHENURA PANENGE PASHAISHWA KANA KUTI PAPISWA MOTO/ THE CLEANSING PROCEDURES WHEN A SACRED PLACE HAS BEEN DEFILED OR WHEN A WILDFIRE OR SUCH A DISASTER HAPPENS

Sabhuku anotaurirwa / Sabhuku, the village headman is told
Sabhuku anotaurira Mambo / Sabhuku tells Mambo, the Chief
Sabhuku otaura kuti: Ndiani munhu adaro? Anodikanwa kuti achenure nyika. / The Sabhuku asks: Who is the person who did this? They are required to cleanse the land.
Anodaidzwa kuti abike doro ne nyama yembuzi svipa / The person responsible is instructed to brew ritual beer and to kill a black goat.
Doro racho, re7 days, rinobikwa musango, kunzvimbo yaakanganisa. / The beer is a seven-day beer. It is brewed in nature, at the place where the offence occurred.
Munhu atadza wacho anotsvaga vanhu vakuru vano mubikira doro / The person who committed the offence finds elders who will brew the beer on their behalf.
Doro rino bikwa nevanhu vakuru, vaguma kubereke / The beer is brewed by elders who have completed childbearing.
Munhu wacho otaurira Sabhuku kana doro raibva / When the beer is ready, the person who committed the offence informs the Sabhuku.
Kana doro raibva Sabhuku naMambo vanotaura zvinodiwa, kuti munhu wacho abika doro ochibvisa chakati kuchenura nyika yedu / Once the beer is ready, the Sabhuku and the Mambo explain what must be done: the person has brewed this beer must make the required offering in order to cleanse our land.
Doro rinodaidzwa vanhu venharaunda, romirwa panzvimbo raka kanganiswa / Members of the surrounding community are called, and the beer is drunk at the place that was desecrated.
Munhu akatadza anonwawo / The person who committed the offence also drinks the beer.
Hapasvike mazera madiki vanochererwa doro vonwa kure nepanzvimbo, panzvimbo panonwa vanhu vakuru vaguma kubereka / Younger people do not enter the cleansing ceremony. Beer is poured for them and they drink it away from the site. At the sacred place itself, it is the elders who have completed childbearing who drink.
Sabhuku ndivo vanodira doro racho / The Sabhuku pours the ritual beer as an offering.
Panenge pakanganiswa pari pane mhepo dzemvura dzinenge dzagumbuka panoshandiswa huku chena. Zvinhu zvinonyanya kushandiswa ipapo zvinenge zviri zvichena - huku chena, mbudzi chena, sadza remutserwa mutsetsiwa/ waka chechiwa, machira machena / When the desecrated place is associated with water spirits (mhepo dzemvura), who have become angry, a white chicken is used. The things used there are predominantly white: a white chicken, a white goat, sadza made from mutserwa, and white cloths.
Wafamba nemunhu, matorwa nechawhiwhi hamuchawone nzira, munogara pamunogara motokumbira vadzimu kutikushaisha. Muno kumbira ruregerero muri mudondo macho. Munovhurirwa nzira. / If you are walking with someone and are caught by mist (chamupupuri/chawhiwhi), and you can no longer see or find the path, you stay where you are and ask the ancestors to forgive you for whatever you may have done wrong. You ask for forgiveness while you are still there in the forest. The path will then be opened for you.
Kana marasika murimunzira, munogona kutora fodya moisa pashizha mogara pasi pemuti motaura, mokumbira kuti marasika kuti muvhurirwe nzira. Kana musina fodya, moshandisa mavhu kana gwenanguruve kana mudembesese. / If you become lost while travelling, you can take tobacco, place it on a leaf and sit underneath a tree. You speak and explain that you are lost, and ask for the path to be opened for you. If you do not have tobacco, you can use soil or plants such as gwenanguruve or mudembesese.
Kana munhu atorwa nenjuzu vasara hatichemi kusvikira chivanhu chaitwa kuti abude. Panoshandiswa bute nekutamba ngoma. Panotsvagwa nyamusoro. / If a person is taken by a njuzu (water spirit), those who remain behind do not cry or mourn until the appropriate customary rituals have been performed for the person to return. These rituals include snuff (bute) and the playing of drums. A nyamusoro, a traditional medium/diviner, is sought.
Pakapera cyclone hapana kubikwa doro, nyika yakasviba. / After the cyclone, no ritual beer was brewed; the land is unclean/ tainted (nyika yakasviba).
Makore ese, tinobika doro ratinobika: / Every year, we brew ritual beer
Tokumbira mvura / We ask for rain.
Tokumbira kuti mhepo risanyaye / We ask that the winds do not become too strong or destructive.
Tokumbira mvura rakanaka / We ask for good rains.
Rinobikwa panguva pano, yekuti takuda kuzotanga kurima. Vatanga kurima asi havadyare mbeu kanadoro risati rabikwa. Doro rinenge riri rekukumbira mvura. / It is brewed around this time, when we are preparing to begin cultivating the fields. People may have begun preparing their fields, but they do not sow their seed before the ritual beer has been brewed. The beer is brewed specifically to ask for rain.

Kana matsime akawoma zvinoitwa sei: / What is done when the springs dry up?
Moenda kunaSabhuku motaura kuti mvura yedu haichafambi zvakanaka / You go to the Sabhuku and say: Our water is no longer flowing properly.
Sabhuku anoziva kwaanoenda kunokumbirwa, asi: / The Sabhuku knows where to go and to whom the request must be made.
Panowoma tsime unenge awomesa, vari pasi kana vari mumvhra vanowona akanganisa. Kuchenura kunoitwa nemunhu akakanganisa ipapo. / When a spring dries up, it is understood that someone has caused it to dry up. Those who are beneath the earth or within the water can see who committed the offence. The cleansing must be carried out by the person who committed the offence at that place.
Vakasazikwana hazviiti. Pamatsime mvura inozoramba kudzoka chaizvo chaizvo. / If the responsible person does not come forward, it cannot be resolved. At springs in particular, the water may completely refuse to return.
Vakabuda pachena zvinoita. / If the person comes forward openly, then the problem can be resolved.
Matsime akacementerwa, mvura yakabuda, yakatiza pakacementerwa, kwa kubuda hekubanzi. Mvura inotiza kana kubuda nepasi pasi kuti musaitevere. / Some springs were cemented over, and when this happened the water moved away. It fled from the place that had been cemented and began emerging somewhere else. Water may escape or begin flowing underground so that you cannot follow it.
Zvimwe zvatinowunzirwa nehurumende zvekucementa matsime vachiti varikuitira hygiene, ivo vari mumvura vanenge vachiti itsvina. / Some of the things brought to us by the government, such as cementing springs in the name of hygiene, are regarded differently by those who dwell within the water: to them, this is pollution or dirt.
Ndosaka kana muchiwona kana mapombi emvura haadi kuenda achiiswa pamuromo panobuda mvura. Unoenda 20-30m, kuitira kuti inenge yambofamba. / This is also why, when installing water pipes, the pipes should not be placed directly at the mouth of the spring where the water emerges. They should be placed approximately 20-30 metres away, so that the water has first had an opportunity to flow naturally.

Pakuvaka bridge:
Mhepo dzemvura hadzidi mushonga / The water spirits (mhepo dzemvura) do not want medicines/chemicals (mushonga).
Mabridge pakutanga, panoda kubikwa doro / Before bridges are built, ritual beer must first be brewed.
Panobuda munhu anobudirwa ne mashavi emvura, otaura kuti pano ndinoda zvakati, wunganidzai zvakati, mbudzi chena, huku chena nesadza re mutserwa. / A person through whom the water spirits manifest may emerge and say:
Here, I require these things. Gather these things: a white goat, a white chicken and sadza made from mutserwa.
Doro rikaibva, zvinhu zvoshandiswa, bridge rovakwa. / When the ritual beer is ready, the required things are used/offered, and the bridge can then be built.
Mhepo dzemvura dzinoda kukumbirwa. / The water spirits must first be asked for permission.

Testimony:
See this ranger, right? There was another ranger who was in love with his girlfriend. So this river, which we are hearing and The Musapa River, they meet just close by at the confluence. There is a nice beautiful dam. It is a sacred place. 
So, these guys, they came out of the camp and went to that spot. From them speaking, the ey made love at that place. So as they said it themselves, they did not know what happened, as they themselves explained it. It seems as if they were swallowed, and all their clothes were taken away.
Just after that spring, you know the spring without water? Just below there. That is where they came out.
The two of them came out naked. The guy had lost his private parts.
An old lady saw them and covered them up. They came to the Chief. It was a serious issue. Even the Chief had no solution for such an occurrence, so he invited some spiritual people to come and assist with how to deal with the issue.
There were about four spirit mediums. They were invited by the Chief while the couple were at the Chief's place. They were asked to carry out a cleansing ceremony, kuchenura.
Each person was given a task: You, the man, do this. You, the woman, do this.
But the man was very stubborn. Even while these things were happening, he was very stubborn and was not complying.
So the woman prepared for the ceremony at the place. But the man said: I am going. I am leaving. He was from Maputo. He just said: I am going. I will find a doctor, and then he left.
Two weeks later, he died.
The woman survived. She is still alive. She is from Chikukwa.

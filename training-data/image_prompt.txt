Create a speculative photographic collage rooted in Chikukwa and Chimanimani, Zimbabwe, in which water connects land, plants, community and possible ecological futures. Let water cosmologies, shifting landscapes, ecological reciprocity, ancestral memory and youth perspectives suggest specific relationships, rather than generic fantasy symbols.

Use the selected 2–10 local research files to guide at least two newly generated environmental fragments and the composition. Translate relevant passages into concrete subjects and spatial relationships: interlocking water and soil, recurring contours, changes of scale, submerged thresholds or regeneration. Record which sources inform each research-led fragment and the arrangement. Keep community testimony, scholarship and speculative interpretation distinct; do not reconstruct withheld rituals or invent sacred identities.

Randomly choose at least two general Chikukwa photographs and two water photographs as generation references, increasing these numbers for larger compositions. Include sacred-water photographs in the eligible reference pool. Use exactly one randomly selected collage reference to guide composition only. Never paste any of these reference photographs directly.

When people are enabled, use direct crops from at least two distinct archive people photographs, normally four and at most eight. Preserve whole visible people or groups and their original appearance. Never generate, recolour or transform people.

Crop every fragment according to its role in the composition. Use visible collage edges, purposeful scale shifts and interlocking environmental pieces; no uncut photographs, dominant full-canvas image or hidden stack of fragments. Keep a deep water-green or earth ground beneath cuts, with no white or pale canvas gaps. Use only checked treatments, sparingly, on generated material; leave most fragments photographic.
